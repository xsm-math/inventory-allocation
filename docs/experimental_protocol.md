# Experimental protocol

## Inputs and demand generation

The source table `data/channels.csv` contains two products and five channels per product. Channel forecasts, capacities, margins, and costs are synthetic assumptions. Products are solved separately with no substitutable inventory or shared financial constraint.

For scenario $n$ and channel $i$, demand is generated as

$$
D_i^{(n)}\mid A^{(n)},B_i^{(n)}\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\beta\widehat d_i A^{(n)}B_i^{(n)}),
$$

where $\log A^{(n)}$ and $\log B_i^{(n)}$ are independent normal variables with mean $-\sigma^2/2$ and variance $\sigma^2$. The common factor $A^{(n)}$ induces channel dependence. Each multiplier has mean one, giving $\mathbb E[D_i]=\beta\widehat d_i$. The reference setting uses $\sigma=0.30$ and $\beta=1$.

Planning uses 400 scenarios with seed 42. Evaluation uses 2,000 scenarios with seed 10042. The same evaluation sample is used for all policies within a case. Seeds are reused across products and sensitivity settings, so these cases are not independent replications for pooled inference.

## Policy construction

All policies first satisfy $\ell_i=\lceil0.25\widehat d_i\rceil$ and respect channel capacity. Proportional allocation applies forecast weights to residual stock after the lower bounds; it is not necessarily proportional in final total quantities. Margin priority uses margin net of shipping and disregards stochastic saturation. The optimized policy uses empirical marginal values from planning demand only.

The reference stocks are 620 and 780 units for Camera-A and Gimbal-B, respectively. In the sensitivity grid, stock is the integer part of aggregate forecast multiplied by 0.5, 0.7, 0.9, or 1.1. Test-demand multipliers are 0.7, 1.0, and 1.3. Planning demand remains at multiplier 1.0 throughout. The grid comprises 24 product/stock/demand cases and 72 policy evaluations.

## Measurements

| Quantity | Definition |
|---|---|
| Economic value | Sales margin minus shipping, channel holding costs, and shortage penalties. |
| Contribution | Economic value before shortage penalties. |
| Fill rate | Total sold units divided by total demand across test scenarios. |
| Shortage | Mean unserved demand per scenario. |
| Channel leftovers | Mean dispatched units remaining unsold per scenario. |
| Central stock | Inventory not dispatched; deterministic for a fixed policy. |
| Fifth-percentile value | Empirical 0.05 quantile of scenario economic value. |

For paired differences $Z_n=V_n^{\mathrm{opt}}-V_n^{\mathrm{prop}}$, the reported interval is

$$
\overline Z\ \pm\ 1.96\,s_Z/\sqrt{2000}.
$$

It measures approximate Monte Carlo uncertainty conditional on the policy and generator. It excludes parameter uncertainty, forecast misspecification, and variability across planning samples. The downside quantile is descriptive and is not an optimized risk criterion.

## Output files

`metrics.csv` records reference-case performance; `allocations.csv` records channel quantities; `paired_gain.json` records paired means and interval half-widths; `sensitivity.csv` records the full grid. `comparison.svg` plots reference mean economic value. All are regenerated by `python experiment.py`.

## Interpretation

The objective weighs served units differently across channels. Higher economic value can therefore coexist with a lower aggregate fill rate or larger leftovers. The reference experiment exhibits this trade-off for both products. Claims about empirical business performance would require observed demand, calibrated economic parameters, and evaluation across operational periods.
